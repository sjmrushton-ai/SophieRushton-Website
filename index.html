SOPHIE RUSHTON ART

Every pattern begins somewhere small.

A line that repeats. A shape that branches. A rhythm that almost holds, then shifts.

Soph Rushton is an artist working between Amsterdam and London, exploring how patterns emerge from living systems. Her work starts with a simple question: how does nature organise itself? Not just how things look, but how they grow, repeat, and evolve over time.

In nature, nothing is random, but nothing is perfectly ordered either.

Branching structures split and resplit. Cells divide. Spirals follow mathematical rules like the Fibonacci sequence, yet never unfold in exactly the same way twice. Soph translates these systems into visual form, building patterns through repetition, symmetry, and gradual transformation. Each piece develops its own internal logic, structured but never static.

But these kinds of pattern systems aren’t only biological.

Across cultures, pattern has long functioned as a language.

Indigenous textile traditions such as Amazonian kene and Andean tocapu encode knowledge through geometry, mapping identity, ecology, and cosmology into woven form. These are not decorative motifs. They are systems for storing and transmitting meaning.

That knowledge is becoming harder to hold onto.

Urban migration pulls younger generations away from traditional practices. Industrial production replaces slow, manual craft. Tourism markets often flatten complex visual languages into simplified aesthetics. At the same time, climate change and deforestation threaten the plants and fibres these traditions depend on.

What gets lost isn’t just technique, it’s a way of understanding the world.

This sits quietly underneath Soph’s work.

Her practice centres on creating bespoke patterns for individuals, pieces that function as personal visual languages. Initials, symbols, and motifs are woven into structured compositions that reflect memory, identity, and connection. No two patterns resolve in the same way, because no two lives do.

Working across ink, print, embroidery, and gold work, Soph creates pieces that carry rhythm, story, and a sense of continuity, patterns that don’t just fill space, but hold meaning within it.
